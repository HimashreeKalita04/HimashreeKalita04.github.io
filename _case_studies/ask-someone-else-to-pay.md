> **TL;DR** — A research-led product I drove from ideation to launch: a payment method for users who'd *never transacted online* — older readers and women in tier-2/3 India — letting someone they trust complete the purchase for them. It now drives **5% of monthly first-time premium purchases**, a segment we'd previously failed to convert at all.

## Context

I'd joined the Monetization team with a single goal: grow **first-time paying users**. But Pratilipi was a mature app post-budget-cuts — the standard levers (discounts, more upsell surfaces) were largely tapped. Squeezing the existing paying funnel harder wasn't going to move the number. I needed a segment, not a tactic.

## Problem

A large part of Pratilipi's most engaged readership — **older users and women in tier-2/3 cities** — loved the content but **had never made an online transaction**. They didn't lack intent or money; they lacked comfort and access to digital payments. Every payment flow we had silently assumed the reader was the payer. For this segment, that assumption was the wall.

## What I did

- **Started from research, not features.** I ran multiple surveys and user-research calls across non-paying users specifically to understand *why* they hadn't paid — and a clear pattern emerged in this digitally-underserved segment.
- **Validated the pattern** before building, then **aligned every stakeholder** (content, growth, payments, leadership) around investing in it — non-trivial when the segment looks "small" on a spreadsheet.
- **Designed "ask someone else to pay"** — the engaged reader initiates the purchase, but a trusted person (often a younger family member) completes the actual transaction, so the reader gets premium without having to navigate online payments themselves.
- **Owned it end-to-end from ideation to launch** — the problem framing, the validation, the flow design, and the rollout were all mine.

## Impact

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Share of monthly first-time premium purchases via this method | **5%** |
| Segment unlocked | Users who had **never transacted in-app before** |
| Strategic value | Turned an "unmonetizable" audience into a recurring revenue contributor + broadened access |

## What I learned

- This is the case study I point to for **product sense.** The win came from refusing to accept "these users don't pay" and instead asking *what would have to be true* for them to pay. The answer wasn't a discount — it was removing themselves from the payment step.
- **Inclusion and monetization aren't in tension.** Designing for a marginalized segment created a new revenue line *and* expanded who got to enjoy the product.
- Conviction needs evidence. Getting stakeholders to fund a "small" segment required walking in with research, a validated pattern, and a clear thesis — not just a hunch.
